# Mycology Resources

## Knowledge

- [OpenStax Biology 2e: Characteristics of Fungi](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/24-1-characteristics-of-fungi)
  A reviewed introductory account of fungal cells, hyphae, mycelium, and nutrition. Use for foundational biology.
- [OpenStax Biology 2e: Classifications of Fungi](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/24-2-classifications-of-fungi)
  The five fungal groups and how each reproduces. Use for: life cycles, mating, gills vs sacs, fairy rings.
- [OpenStax Biology 2e: Ecology of Fungi](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/24-3-ecology-of-fungi)
  Decomposers, mycorrhizae, lichens, parasites. Use for: fungal lifestyles and why they matter.
- [OpenStax Biology 2e: Importance of Fungi in Human Life](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/24-5-importance-of-fungi-in-human-life)
  Decomposers in nutrient cycling, fermentation, and fungal secondary metabolites (antibiotics like penicillin). Use for: microbial competition and applied fungal biology.
- Wikipedia species pages ([Boletus edulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boletus_edulis), [Pleurotus ostreatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleurotus_ostreatus))
  Medium trust; cite only claims backed by their references. Use for: per-species lifestyle and cultivation status.
- [Wikipedia: Fungiculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungiculture)
  Medium trust overview of spawn, pasteurization vs. sterilization, pinning, and fruiting phases. Use for: general cultivation terminology.
- [Cornell Small Farms: Growing Mushrooms for Fun and Profit](https://smallfarms.cornell.edu/2010/04/growing-mushrooms-for-fun-and-profit/)
  Cornell Cooperative Extension overview of shiitake and other mushrooms grown outdoors on logs or woodchips. Useful for cultivation concepts, but not an indoor-growing guide.
- [Cornell Small Farms: Indoor Production](https://smallfarms.cornell.edu/projects/mushrooms/indoor-production/)
  Cornell Cooperative Extension overview of indoor spawn run (incubation) vs. pinning and fruiting environments. Use for: colonization vs. fruiting conditions.
