Hypha anatomy

From Lesson 5.

Growth

Tip growth: a hypha extends only at its tip. The Spitzenkörper (vesicle cluster at the tip) delivers new membrane and wall material; turgor (internal water pressure) pushes outward.

Behind the tip: septa form. Branching: a tip splits, or a new tip sprouts from an older hypha.

Parts

Cell wall: chitin + glucans; strength and protection.

Membrane: stabilised by ergosterol (animals: cholesterol).

Septa (setti): cross-walls, usually perforated so nutrients flow. Absent in coenocytic hyphae (one multinucleate cell, e.g. bread moulds). Gilled mushrooms and porcini are septate.

Feeding

Exoenzymes released outside → external digestion → absorption over the large mycelial surface. Needs moisture (also for turgor).

Regrowth

Hyphal fragments can start new colonies (fragmentation).
