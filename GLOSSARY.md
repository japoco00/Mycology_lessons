# Mycology Glossary

Canonical terms for this workspace: fungal biology, with a bias toward mushroom-forming fungi and home cultivation.

## Terms

**Spore**:
A tiny reproductive cell released by a fungus that can germinate into new hyphae.
_Avoid_: Seed

**Hypha** (plural: hyphae):
A single thread-like filament of a fungus that grows at its tip.
_Avoid_: Root, strand

**Mycelium**:
The network of hyphae; the main, mostly hidden body of the fungus that feeds on its substrate.
_Avoid_: Roots, the fungus's "roots"

**Fruiting body**:
A spore-producing structure grown by the mycelium. A mushroom is one kind of fruiting body.
_Avoid_: Using "mushroom" to mean the whole fungus

**Mating type**:
A compatibility group. Two mycelia must be of different, compatible mating types to fuse.
_Avoid_: Male/female, sex

**Primary mycelium**:
Mycelium grown from one spore; one nucleus per cell. In gilled mushrooms it can't fruit.

**Secondary mycelium**:
Mycelium formed after two compatible primary mycelia fuse; two separate nuclei per cell. The stage that fruits.

**Gills**:
The plates under a mushroom's cap, made of packed hyphae, where spores are produced.

**Chitin**:
The tough polysaccharide in fungal cell walls (also in insect exoskeletons).

**Septum** (plural: septa):
A cross-wall dividing a hypha into compartments, usually with pores so nutrients flow through. Absent in *coenocytic* hyphae (one multinucleate cell).

**Spitzenkörper**:
A cluster of vesicles at a hypha's tip that delivers new membrane and wall material, driving tip growth.

**Exoenzymes**:
Enzymes a fungus releases outside its body to digest food before absorbing it.

**Substrate**:
The dead organic material a fungus grows through and digests (such as wood, straw, sawdust, or soil litter).

**Pasteurization**:
Mild heat treatment (unlike full sterilization) that knocks back fast-growing moulds and pests in substrate while sparing helpful heat-tolerant microbes.

**Pinning** (and **primordia**):
The moment mycelium shifts from feeding to reproducing by knotting hyphae into tiny *primordia* ("pins") that swell into mushrooms.

**Secondary metabolites**:
Compounds fungi make to defend territory or deter rivals—including antibiotics like penicillin—rather than for basic day-to-day growth.

## Flagged ambiguities

- **Spawn**: in this workspace, always the cultivation term (starter material already colonized by mycelium), never a verb for "produce". Mycelium *fruits*; it doesn't "spawn" mushrooms.
